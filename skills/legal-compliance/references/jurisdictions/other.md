# Other Jurisdictions

Use this file when a target country is not covered in `latam.md` or `united-states.md`.

## Method to research an unknown country

Search the web for each topic below. Prefer official sources: the data protection authority, the consumer protection authority, and the official law gazette. Record the URL and the date checked.

1. **Data protection law**: name, number, regulator. Does it apply to foreign businesses that target local users?
2. **Legal basis and consent**: is consent required, and in what form? Is proof required?
3. **Privacy notice**: mandatory content and language.
4. **Registration**: must databases or the business be registered with the regulator? Is a local representative or DPO required?
5. **Cookies**: is prior consent required?
6. **International transfers**: rules for sending data to other countries (cloud hosting).
7. **Minors**: age of digital consent.
8. **Breach notification**: who to notify and the deadline.
9. **Consumer law for online sales**: right of withdrawal period, refund deadline, required seller information, rules on unfair terms.
10. **E-commerce and electronic contracts**: are clickwrap agreements and electronic signatures valid?
11. **Language**: must documents be in the local language?
12. **Accessibility**: any legal duty for websites or apps?
13. **Sector rules**: if the product is in a regulated sector.

## European Union (short notes)

Apply when the business is in the EU or offers goods or services to people in the EU, or monitors their behavior.

- **GDPR**: legal basis per purpose, privacy notice (articles 13 and 14), data subject rights within 1 month, breach notice to the authority within 72 hours, Data Processing Agreements with processors, records of processing, EU representative for non-EU businesses (article 27), transfer safeguards (Standard Contractual Clauses or the EU-US Data Privacy Framework). Fines up to EUR 20 million or 4% of global turnover. Digital consent age: 13 to 16 depending on the country.
- **ePrivacy (cookies)**: prior opt-in for all non-necessary cookies. "Reject" as easy as "Accept". No pre-checked boxes. No cookie walls in many countries.
- **Consumer rights**: 14 calendar days right of withdrawal for distance contracts. Refund within 14 days. Digital content: the right is lost only if the consumer gave prior express consent and acknowledged the loss. A "withdrawal function" (button) for online contracts is required from 19 June 2026 under Directive (EU) 2023/2673 (verify national transposition).
- **Digital Services Act**: duties for platforms with user content (notice and action, contact point, terms clarity).
- **European Accessibility Act**: applies from 28 June 2025 to many e-commerce and consumer digital services (micro-enterprises are exempt for services).
- **AI Act**: duties are phased in from 2025 to 2027. Transparency duties for chatbots and AI-generated content.

## United Kingdom (short notes)

- UK GDPR and Data Protection Act 2018, as amended by the Data (Use and Access) Act 2025 (verify which parts are in force). Regulator: ICO.
- PECR for cookies and marketing: opt-in for non-necessary cookies, with some new exemptions for analytics under the 2025 Act (verify).
- Consumer Contracts Regulations 2013: 14-day cancellation period.
- UK representative needed for non-UK businesses that target UK users.

## Other common markets (pointers only)

- **Canada**: PIPEDA (federal) and Quebec Law 25 (strict consent and privacy impact assessments). CASL for marketing emails (opt-in).
- **India**: Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023 and its Rules (phased start from November 2025, verify dates).
- **Australia**: Privacy Act 1988 with 2024 amendments, Australian Consumer Law.
- **Japan**: APPI. **South Korea**: PIPA. **China**: PIPL (strict transfer rules and local storage in some cases).
