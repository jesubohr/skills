# Legal Documents

Contents and rules for each document. Adapt each one to the real business. Do not copy generic templates without changes: a policy that describes data uses the product does not have, or misses ones it has, is a violation by itself.

Every document needs at the top: title, version, effective date, last updated date, and the legal name of the business. Keep old versions (see `consent-records.md`).

## Terms and Conditions

1. **Parties and acceptance**: legal name, ID, address, contact. How the user accepts (checkbox at registration). Minimum age.
2. **Service description**: what the product does and does not do.
3. **Accounts**: registration rules, user duty to keep credentials safe, account suspension and termination causes and process.
4. **Prices and payments**: currency, taxes, payment methods, billing cycle, price change notice.
5. **Subscriptions** (if any): free trial terms, auto-renewal, how to cancel (must be as easy as signing up in many US states and in consumer laws), what happens at cancel.
6. **Refunds and right of withdrawal**: summary and link to the Refund Policy.
7. **Acceptable use**: prohibited conduct.
8. **User content** (if any): ownership stays with the user, license to the business, takedown process.
9. **Intellectual property**: the business owns the product, brand, and content. Limited license to the user.
10. **Third-party services**: links, integrations, payment providers.
11. **AI features** (if any): outputs can be wrong, user must check them, how inputs are used.
12. **Warranties and limitation of liability**: consumer laws in Latin America limit what you can exclude. Abusive clauses ("cláusulas abusivas") are void. Do not write clauses that remove consumer rights given by law. In the US, use conspicuous text (often capital letters) for disclaimers.
13. **Changes to the terms**: how users are notified and when changes apply.
14. **Governing law and disputes**: the law of the business country, but consumers keep the protection of the law of their own country in most jurisdictions. Arbitration and class action waivers are common in the US but are often void for consumers in Latin America. Mention the consumer protection authority of each country where required (for example SIC in Colombia, PROFECO in Mexico, Procon in Brazil).
15. **Contact and complaints**: channel and response time. Some countries require a complaints book (for example "Libro de Reclamaciones" in Peru).

## Privacy Policy

Many Latin American laws call this the "Política de Tratamiento de Datos" (Colombia) or "Aviso de Privacidad" (Mexico). Use the local name in the local language.

1. **Controller identity**: legal name, ID, address, email, phone. Data protection officer if any.
2. **Data collected**: by category, from the `data-inventory.md` table. Include data collected automatically (IP, device, cookies) and from third parties.
3. **Purposes**: one per use. Separate required purposes from optional ones (marketing).
4. **Legal basis** per purpose (consent, contract, legal duty, legitimate interest where the law allows it).
5. **Sensitive data and minors**: what, why, and that answering questions about sensitive data is optional where the law says so.
6. **Recipients**: processors and third parties by category or name, and why.
7. **International transfers**: countries (for example the US for most cloud providers) and the safeguards used.
8. **Retention**: period per category, or the criteria.
9. **User rights**: access, correction, deletion, objection, portability, revoke consent, opt-out of sale or sharing, and others the local law gives (for example the "ARCO" rights in Mexico). How to exercise them, the legal response deadline, and the right to complain to the regulator (name and link).
10. **Security measures**: general description.
11. **Cookies**: summary and link to the Cookies Policy.
12. **Automated decisions and AI**: if used, the logic in plain words and the right to human review where the law gives it.
13. **Sale or sharing of data** (US): disclosures that state laws require, and the "Do Not Sell or Share" link if applicable.
14. **Changes to the policy**: how users are notified.
15. **Effective date and version**.

## Refund Policy

Base rule from the user: **5 days after purchase**. Replace it with the legal minimum when the law of any target country gives more. Explain the difference between:

- **Right of withdrawal** ("derecho de retracto", "derecho de arrepentimiento", "direito de arrependimento"): a legal right for remote purchases (online, phone). The consumer can cancel without giving a reason, within a legal period. Usually cannot be removed by contract.
- **Warranty** ("garantía legal"): the product must work as offered. This is a separate legal right with its own period. A refund policy cannot remove it.
- **Voluntary refund policy**: anything the business offers beyond the law.

Content:

1. Who can ask for a refund and the period (days, business or calendar, counted from purchase or delivery).
2. How to ask: channel, information needed. Make it as easy as buying. Argentina requires a visible "Botón de arrepentimiento" on the home page.
3. Conditions for physical goods: state of the product, packaging, who pays return shipping.
4. Exceptions allowed by law (for example custom goods, perishables, digital content already accessed with the user's prior consent, services already fully delivered). Only list exceptions the local law allows.
5. Refund method and legal deadline to return the money (for example within 30 calendar days in Colombia).
6. Subscriptions: what happens with the current period at cancel. Pro-rated refunds or not.
7. App store purchases: refunds go through Apple or Google rules.
8. Warranty claims and the link to the consumer authority.

## Other documents to consider

Recommend these when they apply:

- **Acceptable Use Policy**: products with user content or APIs.
- **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)**: B2B products that process data of the customer's users.
- **Shipping Policy**: physical goods.
- **Accessibility statement**: recommended, especially for the US market (ADA lawsuits).
- **Copyright / DMCA Policy**: products with user content.
- **Community guidelines**: social products.
- **Information about the seller**: e-commerce laws in several countries require the seller's legal name, ID, address, and contact on the site (for example Brazil Decreto 7.962/2013).

## Footer and placement

- Links to all policies in the footer of every page and in the app settings screen.
- Links next to the register checkboxes and at checkout.
- Refund policy linked at checkout, before payment.
- The consumer protection authority logo or link where the local law requires it.
